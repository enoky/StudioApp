This stage has two halves: setting up your own business properly, and making sure every song and episode you work on has clear ownership. This is general information, not legal advice. Have an Ontario lawyer and accountant check your setup.

## Your business in Ontario

**Structure.** Most studios start as a **sole proprietorship** (simple and cheap; income goes on your personal tax return) or a **partnership** with a written partnership agreement. Incorporating, provincially or federally, can limit personal liability and change how you're taxed, but it costs more to set up and run. Ask an accountant which fits your income and risk.

**Business name.** If you operate under a name that isn't your own legal name, you must register it through the **Ontario Business Registry**. Registering a sole proprietorship or general partnership costs **$60**.

**HST.** Register once taxable revenue passes **$30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters**. Ontario's rate is 13%.

**Hiring.** Most Ontario businesses with employees must register with **WSIB within 10 calendar days of hiring their first employee**. Ontario's general **minimum wage is $17.95 an hour from October 1, 2026**. Unpaid internships are generally not allowed in Ontario unless they're part of an approved school program, so check before offering one. Freelance engineers and editors should be genuinely independent, with a written contractor agreement.

**Insurance.** Gear (including client gear in your care), general liability, and business interruption. Tell the insurer what's on your walls.

## Every song has two copyrights

From Donald Passman's *All You Need to Know About the Music Business*:

| Copyright | Usually owned by | Collected in Canada by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The composition** (music and lyrics) | Songwriters and their publishers | SOCAN (performance) and CMRRA (mechanical/reproduction) |
| **The master** (the recording) | The artist, or their label | Distributors and labels; Re:Sound (neighbouring rights); CONNECT (reproduction of sound recordings) |

**News for 2026:** SOCAN completed its acquisition of CMRRA on September 1, 2026, so performing and mechanical rights for songs in Canada now sit under one organization. Registration processes are changing, so check both sites for the current steps.

**Neighbouring rights.** Re:Sound splits these royalties between performers and makers (master owners). An independent artist who owns their master can claim both shares. Remind clients to register.

## Where your studio fits

You can earn from the same song in up to four ways:

1. **Session or engineering fee.** Non-recoupable, and usually work-for-hire. You don't own the recording.
2. **Producer fee or advance.** In industry language, a fee is never recouped and an advance is.
3. **Producer points** on the master, a percentage of master income, usually paid only after recording costs are recouped.
4. **Publishing share,** only if you actually helped write the song.

Most of your clients self-release, so write points as a percentage of the artist's net master receipts, with statements and audit rights. Otherwise "3 points" means nothing.

## Paperwork to have ready

- **Booking terms:** deposit, cancellation, overtime, file retention, and who owns the files (normally the client, once paid in full).
- **Split sheet,** signed on the day the song is written: every writer, their share, publisher and SOCAN details. Add a line for master ownership.
- **Producer agreement:** fee or advance, points and how they're calculated, credit, sample responsibility, statements.
- **Contractor agreement** for freelance engineers, so they have no claim on client masters.
- **Guest release** for podcasts.
- **Sample clearance:** the client confirms they'll clear both the master and the composition before release.
