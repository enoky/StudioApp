Creative income is lumpy. Bookings are seasonal, sponsors pay late, and royalties arrive quarterly or later. Cash is what keeps the doors open while you wait.

## The numbers that matter

- **Utilization:** booked hours ÷ available hours. The core health number for any room.
- **Effective hourly rate:** total session revenue ÷ hours booked. Discounts, block deals and free "quick fixes" pull it below your rate card.
- **Break-even hours:** monthly fixed costs ÷ effective hourly rate. Add the pay you want for yourself to get your real target.

### Worked example (estimates)

Rent of $2,500 plus about $700 for utilities, insurance and software comes to $3,200 a month. At an effective $60 an hour, you need about **53 booked hours a month** just to cover that. To also pay yourself $4,000, it's **120 hours a month, roughly 28 a week, every week.**

## Profit First: take your cut before the studio spends it

Mike Michalowicz's insight is about behaviour: we spend what we see. So flip the formula. Move profit, your pay and tax out of reach the moment money arrives, and make the studio run on what's left.

| Account | What it's for |
| --- | --- |
| Income | Every deposit lands here first |
| Profit | A small percentage of every deposit, as a reward and a reserve. Keep it at a second bank |
| Owner's pay | Your wage for doing the sessions |
| Tax | Income tax, set aside so it never surprises you. Second bank |
| Operating expenses | Rent, software, repairs, freelancers, marketing. This is all the studio gets |
| HST (once registered) | Every dollar of HST you collect. It's the government's money |

**Starting split for a new business** (from the book): profit 1%, owner's pay 50%, tax 15%, and the rest for expenses. Move a few points toward the targets each quarter. For a studio under $250,000 in real revenue, the book's target leaves about **30% for operating expenses**. That one line is a hard limit on rent and gear payments.

The 15% tax figure is the book's American default. Ask a Canadian accountant what your effective rate is, including CPP on self-employment income and any instalments.

**Rhythm:** on the 10th and 25th, move money from Income to the other accounts and pay bills. Each quarter, take half of the Profit account as a reward and leave the rest as a reserve.

## The lease test

This is the most useful calculation in the whole guide. Required real revenue = monthly overhead ÷ 30%.

- $4,000 of overhead ÷ 30% = **$13,333 a month** in real revenue.
- At $60 an hour, that's about **222 booked hours a month, about 51 a week.**

Run it the other way: if you can honestly book 25 hours a week at $60, your overhead ceiling is about **$1,950 a month, all in**. That rules out a $2,500 lease until bookings prove otherwise. Try it with your numbers in the [break-even tool](../../tools/break-even/).

## HST in Ontario

You must register for GST/HST once your taxable revenue passes **$30,000 in a single calendar quarter, or over four consecutive calendar quarters**. Ontario's HST rate is 13%. Price your rate card knowing that independent artists, unlike businesses, usually can't claim the HST back. Registering early (voluntarily) lets you claim back HST on gear and rent, so ask your accountant whether it makes sense for you.

## Checklist

- [ ] I know my monthly fixed costs, to the dollar
- [ ] I've calculated my break-even hours and the hours needed to also pay myself
- [ ] I've run the lease test on any space I'm considering
- [ ] I've opened separate accounts for income, profit, owner's pay, tax and operating expenses
- [ ] Profit and tax accounts are at a second bank with no card
- [ ] I've set 10th and 25th reminders to allocate money and pay bills
- [ ] I've listed every recurring charge and cut or renegotiated at least 10%
- [ ] I know when I'll have to register for HST, and I've asked an accountant about registering early
