Validation means getting strangers to commit time or money before you commit yours. It's cheap, it's fast, and it's the step most studio founders skip.

## Ask about the past, not your idea

Rob Fitzpatrick's *The Mom Test* is built on one observation: people will lie to you about your idea to be kind. So don't ask about the idea. Ask about their life and what they've already done.

| Instead of… | Ask… |
| --- | --- |
| Would you book a studio like mine? | Walk me through the last time you recorded. Where, how long, who engineered it? |
| How much would you pay per hour? | What did your last single or episode cost you, all in? Who paid? |
| Does this area need a studio? | Where do you and your friends record now? How do you get there? |
| What would your dream studio have? | What went wrong at your last session? What did you do about it? |

Three rules: talk about their life, ask about specific past events, and talk less than they do.

## Ask one scary question

Every conversation should include a question whose answer could kill your plan. For studios these are usually about **money** ("What's the most you've spent on a session?"), **frequency** ("How many times did you record last year?") and **switching** ("What would make you leave your current engineer?").

A lukewarm "meh" is a useful answer. It's honest, and it saves you money. Don't pitch harder to turn it into a compliment.

## Only commitments count

A good conversation ends with facts, or with the other person giving up something they value:

- **Time:** a booked trial date, a call to plan their EP.
- **Reputation:** an introduction to their bandmates or manager, a public testimonial.
- **Money:** a paid deposit, a prepaid block of hours.

"Love it, let me know when you open" is a polite brush-off. "Can we lock in March 14?" is demand.

## Run real sessions in someone else's room

You don't need your own space to validate. Rent hourly time at an existing studio, run the session, and charge the client your rate. You learn what people pay for, how they found you and whether you like the work, without signing a lease.

> **Gate 1:** about **40 hours pre-sold or deposited within 60 days**. If you miss it, change the segment or the offer, not the gear. (This threshold is an estimate for a one-room studio. Set your own with the [break-even tool](../../tools/break-even/).)

## Write your kill criteria now

Decide in advance, while you're calm, what would make you stop or change course. For example: fewer than 10 paying clients after six months of running lean, or three months in a row where the studio can't cover its bills.

## Checklist

- [ ] I've written three big questions I want answered in this round of conversations
- [ ] I've had at least 10 conversations about past behaviour, not about my idea
- [ ] I asked at least one scary question about money, frequency or switching
- [ ] I've run at least one paid session, in my space or a rented room
- [ ] I've collected at least one deposit or prepaid booking from a stranger
- [ ] I've tracked where each paying client heard about me
- [ ] I've written down my Gate 1 target and my kill criteria
