Michael Gerber's *E-Myth Revisited* describes the trap most skilled founders fall into: being great at the work doesn't mean you understand the business of the work. The fix is to build the studio as if you were going to hand it to someone else, even if you never do.

## Systematize everything around the art

Clients often book *you*: your ears, taste and credits. Don't try to systematize the art. Systematize everything around it, so all your attention in the session goes to the music.

| Role | Write these down first |
| --- | --- |
| Booker | Inquiry reply, intake form, quote and package choice, deposit, confirmation and reminder emails |
| Assistant | Room reset, setup by session type (band, vocal, podcast), mic and headphone check, teardown |
| Engineer | Session flow, recall notes, file naming and folders, bounce specs, revision policy |
| Operations | Daily backups, file retention, delivery method and timeline, maintenance calendar |
| Marketing | Testimonial and credit request, content capture (with consent), referral thank-you |
| Finance | 10th and 25th allocations, invoices and deposits, HST filing, monthly numbers review |

Rule of thumb: **write a checklist the second time you do any task.** When someone else can follow it and get your result, that job is ready to hand over.

## Before, during and after a session

**Before**
- Intake call or form: references, goals, who has the final say, what "done" means.
- Ask about headphone mix preferences.
- Test every mic, preamp, DI and headphone feed before the client arrives. Trust is earned in the first ten minutes.

**During**
- Get the headphone mix right before the first take.
- Comfort first, then challenge, and explain why you're pushing.
- Give honest notes without being harsh. Stop and reset when a take isn't working.
- Commit to sounds as you go, and note decisions.

**After**
- Check every file, bounce and link before you send it.
- Send files, notes and next steps the same day.
- Ask for a testimonial and permission to credit them.

## Files and backups

- **3-2-1 rule:** three copies, on two kinds of storage, with one off-site.
- A standard folder structure and file naming for every client.
- A written file retention period (for example, 12 months), stated in your booking terms.
- Keep every draft mix. Clients often go back to an earlier version.

## The weekly numbers

Track these every week in a simple spreadsheet:

- Inquiries, and the inquiry-to-booking rate
- Booked hours and utilization (booked ÷ available)
- Effective hourly rate
- Rebook rate
- Deposits collected, and the balance in each Profit First account

## Protect yourself

Late sessions are part of the job, but burnout ends studios. Schedule days off and protect them. Plan focused work at about half your hours; the rest goes to email, admin, sales and thinking.

## Checklist

- [ ] I have a written setup and teardown checklist for each session type
- [ ] I use an intake form or call for every new client
- [ ] I check every deliverable before sending it
- [ ] I have 3-2-1 backups running
- [ ] I have a standard folder structure and a written file retention period
- [ ] I track inquiries, booked hours, effective rate and rebooks every week
- [ ] I've scheduled regular days off
