Clients don't buy hours. They buy a radio-ready single, a finished episode, or a demo good enough to send to a booker. Price the outcome, and use hours to protect your margin.

## Find the market rate

Don't guess and don't copy one competitor. Collect five or more current rate cards from studios your clients could choose instead: around Perth, Smiths Falls and Carleton Place, and in Ottawa and Kingston. Note what's included: engineer, mixing, revisions, file delivery, parking. City studios often charge more but cost clients a long drive, which is part of your pitch. Then place yourself deliberately. Lower than average needs a reason (you're new and building credits). Higher needs proof (credits, a great room, before-and-after audio).

## A simple rate card

| Offer | How it's priced | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hourly session (with engineer) | Per hour, 2-hour minimum | Easy entry point for new clients |
| Day or half-day block | Fixed price, slightly below hourly | Fills the calendar in big chunks |
| Prepaid block of hours | Bundle of 10 or 20 hours, paid upfront | Cash now, loyal clients. Keep the money in a separate "drip" account and count it as earned only as the hours are used |
| Single package | Record, mix and master one song, with 2 revisions | Sells an outcome; easy to compare |
| Mixing only | Per song, 2 revisions included, extras charged per round | Remote clients; protects your time |
| Podcast packages | Per finished episode or monthly retainer | See [Podcasts](../podcast/) |
| Off-peak rate | Weekday daytime discount | Fills hours that would sit empty |

**Remember HST.** Once you're registered, add 13% HST in Ontario. Decide whether your published rates include it, and be consistent.

## Revisions cost real hours

Example (estimates): a $300 mix that takes 6 hours, plus 2 revision rounds of 1.5 hours each, is 9 hours of work. Your effective rate drops from $50 to about $33 an hour. Include a set number of revisions, then charge per round, and put it in writing before the first mix goes out. Senior suggests asking for **audio examples, not adjectives** ("vocal like this track at 0:47"), and having **one spokesperson per band**.

## Deposits and cancellations

- Take a **non-refundable deposit** to hold any booking. 25–50% is common, but check what local studios do.
- Set a **cancellation window**, for example 48 or 72 hours. Inside it, the deposit is kept.
- Spell out **late arrival** (the clock starts at the booked time), **overtime**, **file retention** (how long you keep sessions) and when **masters are released** (once paid in full).

Use the [booking policy template](../../templates/booking-policy/) as a starting point, and have a lawyer review it.

## Handling rate pushback

From Chris Voss's *Never Split the Difference*: don't discount straight away. Name their concern first ("It sounds like the budget for this release is already stretched"), then ask a calibrated question ("What does this single need to do for you this year?"). Trade instead of cutting: a prepaid block, an off-peak slot, fewer revisions, or a lower fee plus points with a signed producer agreement. State your rate calmly, then stop talking.

## Checklist

- [ ] I've collected at least five local rate cards and noted what they include
- [ ] I have a one-page rate card with an entry offer, a package and a block deal
- [ ] Every package says how many revisions are included and what extra rounds cost
- [ ] I take a deposit for every booking
- [ ] I have written cancellation, late-arrival, overtime and file-retention rules
- [ ] I know whether my prices include HST, and my invoices show it correctly
- [ ] I track my effective hourly rate monthly, not just my rate card
