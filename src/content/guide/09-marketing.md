Most bookings come from people who already know you, or who know someone who does. Your job is to make great sessions, make that work visible, and be easy to book.

## The session-to-booking loop

1. **Run a great session.** Prepared, comfortable, honest. The producers in *Behind the Glass* talk far more about psychology and trust than about gear.
2. **Capture the process.** Photos, short clips and notes, with the client's permission.
3. **Share something small,** most days, on the one or two platforms where your clients actually are.
4. **Teach and tell stories.** Turn the best of it into lasting pages on a site you own.
5. **Build reputation.** Credits, testimonials, an email list, your local scene.
6. **Get bookings and referrals,** then back to step 1.

## Show your work, not ads

Austin Kleon's *Show Your Work!* fits a studio unusually well, because every session produces sound, pictures and stories:

- **Share process, not just the product:** the mic you chose for this singer and why, how you built the headphone mix that got the take, a rough versus the final.
- **Teach what you know.** Short tutorials on vocal recording or podcast mic technique. Most viewers will decide they'd rather pay you.
- **Credit everyone,** every time: artist, players, engineers.
- **Own your turf.** Register a domain. Social platforms come and go; your site holds the portfolio, rate card, booking link and email sign-up.
- **Cap it.** About 30 minutes a day is enough. Batch-capture during sessions and post on a schedule.

**Client permission comes first.** Unreleased songs and vulnerable takes aren't yours to post, and even a short clip uses the artist's master and the songwriters' composition. Add a content clause to your booking terms (what you may capture, what needs approval, an opt-out), and post approved clips after release unless agreed otherwise.

## Be a fan first

- Go to shows, open mics, producer meetups and podcast meetups as a participant, not with flyers.
- Promote other local artists and studios. Be the person who connects people.
- Host a listening night or open house in your room.
- Partner with venues, music schools, college programs and local businesses that need podcasts.

## Local channels around Perth

- **Perth & District Chamber of Commerce** and the **Downtown Heritage Perth BIA**, which represents more than 150 downtown businesses. They're the shortest route to local business owners who might want a podcast, ads or audio for their website.
- **Local venues, festivals, music teachers and schools.** Offer demo days, student rates or recordings of live sets.
- **Lanark County and Ottawa music communities** online and in person. Some Ottawa artists will happily drive about an hour for the right room and rate.

## Make it easy to book

- **Google Business Profile** with real photos, hours and reviews from real clients. Never buy reviews or followers.
- **One clear booking path:** a booking link or form, a fast reply (same day), and a deposit to confirm.
- **Ask every client** for a testimonial, permission to credit them, and who else they know who's recording.
- **Use the end of every project** to line up the next one.

## Track what works

Write down where each inquiry came from. After a month, keep the one or two channels that brought paying clients and drop the rest.

## Checklist

- [ ] I have a one-page website with my rate card, booking link and email sign-up
- [ ] I have a Google Business Profile with real photos
- [ ] My booking terms include a content and privacy clause
- [ ] I capture process content (with consent) at every session
- [ ] I've published at least one short tutorial or session story
- [ ] I ask every client for a testimonial, a credit and a referral
- [ ] I go to at least one local show or meetup a week
- [ ] I track where each inquiry came from
