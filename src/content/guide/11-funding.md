The safest money is the money you don't borrow. Start lean, pre-sell, and let bookings pay for upgrades. When you do borrow, borrow for things that are already earning.

## In order of safety

1. **Pre-sales and deposits.** Prepaid blocks and packages are cash and validation at the same time.
2. **Savings you can afford to lose,** kept separate from your personal emergency fund.
3. **Equipment you already own,** and renting time elsewhere instead of building.
4. **Loans,** for proven demand only.
5. **Grants,** a bonus, never the plan. They're competitive, slow and tied to specific activities.

## Loans and financing

**Futurpreneur** lends to entrepreneurs aged 18–39 who are Canadian citizens or permanent residents: up to **$75,000**, made up of up to $25,000 from Futurpreneur and up to $50,000 from BDC, plus up to two years of mentorship. It's a loan you repay with interest, not a grant.

**Canada Small Business Financing Program (CSBFP).** The government shares risk with your bank so it's easier to get a loan. The maximum per borrower is **$1.15 million**, and you apply through a bank or credit union. Lenders take security in what's financed and may also ask for a personal guarantee, so read the terms carefully.

**Equipment financing** from gear retailers or lenders. Keep total payments inside your operating-expenses limit (about 30% of real revenue).

## Grants for music companies

**Ontario Creates: Ontario Music Investment Fund (OMIF).** The fund was restructured for 2026–27. The Music Creation stream was discontinued, and a new **Music Futures** stream invests in smaller, established music companies with revenues between about $25,000 and $125,000. Other streams cover music company development, industry initiatives, live music and global markets. Most 2026–27 deadlines have passed, so watch for the 2027–28 guidelines. Eligibility rules are specific to music companies (labels, publishers, artist managers and similar), so read them before you plan around this.

**Canada Council for the Arts** reorganized its programs in 2025. **Explore and Create** funds the creation of artistic work by artists and groups, which suits artist-run or creative projects more than a commercial studio.

## Grants that help your clients pay you

This is the overlooked one. When your artist clients get funding, some of it can go to studio time:

- **Ontario Arts Council: Music Recording Projects** supports recording by Ontario-based musicians, including composers, songwriters and beat producers. Studio and recording location rental is an eligible expense.
- **FACTOR: Artist Development** offers early-career Canadian artists 75% of eligible expenses up to $5,000 for a year of development activities, including sound recording.

Learn the deadlines, and help clients budget your studio time into their applications. It's real value, and it brings in work.

## Watch for scams

Be wary of anyone who "guarantees" a grant or sponsorship for a fee, pay-to-play showcases, and playlist-placement services that promise streams. Program bodies don't need a broker.

## Checklist

- [ ] I've built a startup budget with one-time costs, monthly costs and runway
- [ ] I've pre-sold or taken deposits before borrowing anything
- [ ] Any loan is for proven demand, and payments fit inside 30% of real revenue
- [ ] I've avoided or capped personal guarantees, and nothing is secured against my home
- [ ] I've checked my eligibility for Futurpreneur or the CSBFP
- [ ] I know the current OMIF streams and whether my business is eligible
- [ ] I know the OAC and FACTOR programs my artist clients can use for studio time
