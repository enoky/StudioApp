Both Eric Nuzum (*Make Noise*) and Glen Weldon (*NPR's Podcast Start Up Guide*) come out of NPR, and they agree that shows rarely fail because of the mic. They fail because nobody can say what the show is, the work takes far longer than expected, and episodes stop coming out. A studio that takes over the editing, producing and mixing solves all three.

## The work clients underestimate

- An interview show can need many hours of host prep per episode, and shows that sound loose are usually heavily edited.
- Listeners value **consistency over frequency**. Clients need banked episodes and a steady pipeline, which is exactly what a monthly retainer provides.
- **The room matters more than the mic.** An expensive USB mic in an echoey room still sounds amateur. Before-and-after audio is your best sales tool.

## A podcast service menu

| Service | What's included | Pricing model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Show development session | 2–3 hours: ten-word description, target listener, format, name check. Client leaves with a one-page show plan | Flat fee |
| Pilot package | 2–3 pilot episodes plus a listening session with peers | Flat fee, credited against a retainer |
| Recording | Local and remote, every voice on its own track | Hourly or bundled |
| Edit and mix retainer | Edit, mix, loudness, show notes, set revision limits | Per episode, billed monthly |
| Launch package | Artwork, trailer, descriptions, transcripts, hosting set up **in the client's name** | Flat fee |
| Grow add-on | Short video clips, ad swaps between your clients, guest exchanges | Monthly |
| Branded podcasts | Strategy, production and a promotion plan for a business | Project fee plus retainer |

## The math: retainer vs room hire (estimates)

A weekly interview show, 75 minutes recorded and 45 published, takes about 6 hours of studio labour per episode: 1.5 hours engineering, 3 editing, 1 mixing and mastering, 0.5 publishing.

| Model | Per episode | Per month |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Room hire only: 1.5 h × $60 | $90 | about $390 |
| Full service: 6 h × $75 | $450 | about $1,950 |

One full-service client is worth about five room-hire clients. The catch is capacity. If one person has about 20 focused editing hours a week, that's roughly 14 episodes a month, or **about 3 weekly shows per person**. Price, staffing and an editing checklist have to be designed together. Model yours in the [podcast money tool](../../tools/podcast-money/).

## What podcast clients can earn (so you can advise them)

At small audiences, ads pay very little. At 800 downloads an episode, a $25 CPM (price per 1,000 downloads) and two ad slots, an episode earns about $40 before any network's cut. Better options at that size are a direct sponsor that serves the exact audience, memberships or bonus episodes, and using the show as a calling card for the host's business. CPMs vary widely, so treat any figure as an assumption and check current rates.

## Paperwork and consent

- Get **consent on tape** at the start of every recording: the guest knows it's being recorded for the podcast.
- Use a **guest release** for publishing rights, especially for sensitive topics or minors. Both books' legal guidance is American, so have an Ontario media lawyer adapt yours.
- Use **precleared music libraries**. There's no automatic "20 seconds is fine" rule.
- Set up hosting **in the client's name** so they own their feed.
